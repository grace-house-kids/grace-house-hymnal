The Sunday Zine

Nothing, except.
Twice in the books of Kings, a prophet walks up to a widow at the end of her rope. Elijah asks one for bread. She tells him she has nothing baked, only a handful of flour and a little oil, enough for one last meal. Elisha asks another what she has in the house. Nothing, she says, except a jar of oil. Both women answer the same way. Nothing. Then, almost as an afterthought, the small thing they had stopped counting. We know that answer because we give it. Ask most of us what we have to offer and we’ll say “not much” before the question is finished. We’ve taken inventory, and the shelves look bare. We rarely ask whether we counted right, or who taught us to count. When we gather, we’ll hear an old story told around the circle. Before you come, sit with a question. If someone knocked on your door tonight and asked what you have in the house, what would you say? And what would you leave off the list?

Mind the Mess
Renovations are underway at the Grace House, so please watch your step and pardon the dust for a while. When the work is done, our worship space will be nearly double its size. The church will also have a large storage area, a walk-out covered patio, and a community tool shed, and the foyer will become our donation center. Every piece of it is meant for making room: room to gather, room to share, and room for whoever comes next.

Farm Day
Last farm day some of us headed back out to Jeremiah and Julie’s to lend a hand. We took down the fence around one of the gardens and rolled it into bundles, then pulled the big tarps off the soil and folded them up for next time. Those tarps matter more than they look. On an organic no-till farm they do the job a tiller would, covering the ground so the weeds die back and the beds are ready for planting without the soil ever being turned. Good work, good company, and a garden ready for its next season.

A Visit to the Food Pantry
Nick and Eli recently went downtown to see the food pantry at work. They got a look at how the operation runs from the inside, how people can get involved, and what they need most, which isn’t always what you’d guess. They’ll share what they learned when we gather. If you’ve been wondering where your donations go or how to do more than drop off a bag, come ready to ask.

Clothing Donations
This week Eli visited the clothing donation center and learned how the whole thing works, from drop-off to the people who end up wearing what we send. He’ll share what he found when we gather. In the meantime, if you have gently used clothes or shoes you’re ready to part with, drop them in the donation box in the Kid’s classroom at the Grace House. We’ll launder everything and get it into the hands of folks who need it. Every item helps.
